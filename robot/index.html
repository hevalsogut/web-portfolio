How it works

  1. Vocal cords. A pulse generator buzzes at the note you press, with breath noise mixed in.
  2. Throat and mouth. The buzz travels down a tube of 44 segments. The width of each segment sets which frequencies ring, which is what makes an “a” sound different from an “i”.
  3. Speech. Text is split into Turkish syllables. For each sound the tongue, lips, jaw and soft palate move to a new shape, and narrow gaps add hiss for s and ş.
